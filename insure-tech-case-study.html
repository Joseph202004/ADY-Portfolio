02 — The challenge

A tool that knew too much — and told you nothing clearly.

OneBuzz uses AI to extract insurance product configurations from BRDs, rate cards and policy wordings, compressing 3–5 days of manual configuration into hours of review. But the interface surfaced errors in four separate places, hid the connection between an extracted value and the document it came from, and confronted users with blocking validation only at the very end.

03 — Who it is for

Domain experts who cannot afford UI friction.

The system serves deep insurance domain experts, not developers. They configure several products a quarter, each across multiple sessions, where a single misread co-payment bracket can cost millions.

Persona 01

Product Manager / Head of Product

Motive — speed-to-market and compliance.
Needs — high-level coverage verification, exclusions, compliance checklists.
Pain — blockers surface only at final pre-publish, costing hours of rework.

Persona 02

Senior Actuarial Analyst

Motive — precision and underwriting safety.
Needs — verify rate cards, co-pays and age bands with total precision.
Pain — source attribution is passive; no live link from a field back to its page.

04 — Eight flaws, eight responses

An audit of the existing interface.

Eight behaviours were undermining trust in the AI-extracted output. The redesign answers each with a specific, scoped affordance — no blanket rewrites.

  1. 01

    Attention fragmentation

    Errors and confidence scores live in four places — panic and skipped steps.

    Response

    A dedicated Issues tab with one-click Resolve; the stepper carries a live "Needs Attention" count.

  2. 02

    Passive source attribution

    "From: BRD" is a text tag — users hunt through five PDFs manually.

    Response

    Every value cites its origin (BRD Pg 2, Sec 4.1); the Document Inspector opens that passage highlighted.

  3. 03

    Blind navigation

    Plan nodes give no signal of how close each one is to done.

    Response

    The active stage expands its own sub-steps with a live progress bar.

  4. 04

    Flat coverage scroll

    37 coverages in one flat list means endless vertical scrolling.

    Response

    Coverages live under collapsible plan accordions — Mini, Medi, Max.

  5. 05

    Competing side drawers

    Tree, Extraction, Issues and Docs tabs fight for the same panel.

    Response

    A context-sensitive split — only the relevant panel surfaces while you are editing.

  6. 06

    Disconnected "Mark Reviewed"

    Save, value, and "Mark Reviewed" are three separate clicks.

    Response

    Save and the "Mark Reviewed (n/151)" counter co-locate; resolving updates the audit in place.

  7. 07

    Post-hoc shock

    Critical errors surface only at the final pre-publish step.

    Response

    Field-level amber and red borders surface weak values inline while editing.

  8. 08

    High-risk friction

    Destructive actions — delete coverage, revert — lack visible feedback.

    Response

    Standardised states: "Draft auto-saved", a visible Revert affordance, an undo path.

05 — Design principles

Four ideas that shaped every decision.

Once the audit was clear, four principles became the rubric for every layout, control and interaction in the rebuild.

  1. 01

    Confidence-coded fields

    Low-confidence values carry amber borders and a 75% badge — risk is visible inline during editing, not at final review.

    Trust
  2. 02

    Inspect → live source split

    Confidence badges open a Document Inspector pinned to the canvas, scrolled to the exact BRD passage with the figure highlighted.

    Evidence
  3. 03

    One multi-tab workspace

    Tree, Extraction, Issues and Docs live in a single right-hand panel — structure, audit, issues and source one click apart.

    Focus
  4. 04

    Staged navigation with sub-steps

    The left rail maps the five stages; the active stage expands to show its own sub-step progress.

    Orientation

06 — The trust loop

The interaction that ties the whole system together.

The user never has to leave the canvas to trust an AI extraction.

  1. 01 — Notice

    An amber 75% bead beside a field signals low confidence. The user scans; they do not search.

  2. 02 — Verify

    One click opens the source passage on the left, highlighted at page 12 of the BRD. The user reads; they do not hunt.

  3. 03 — Resolve

    Accept & Verify turns the bead green. The auditor warning clears. Progress ticks 12/37 → 13/37.

07 — Final designs

Five core surfaces across the unified workspace.

Each component traces back to a specific friction point retired by the audit — extraction trust, source attribution, drill-down navigation and dependency visibility.

08 — The five redesigned screens

01

Configuration method

Three paths — AI extraction as the primary route, template, or manual — with a live file-upload queue.

02

AI extraction & audit

A split-pane source viewer, an 89% extraction-quality audit, and parameter tables with per-row confidence.

03

Unified product workspace

The core three-column screen — coverage navigation, an overview and edit canvas, and the tree/extraction/issues/docs panel.

04

Product review & publish

A pre-publish validator — the checklist of blockers and warnings, plan and coverage status, subsystem health.

05

Stakeholder overview

A read-only shareable dashboard — metric cards plus per-plan limits and member-details tables.
