Adi Hand — the traced fallback, and how it was made

Hi I am Adi
I like to draw
  1. Source. The handwritten A–Z chart, 1102×348px.
  2. Segment. Binarise, label connected components, cluster them into three rows, then cut each row at its widest gaps so the pair count matches the alphabet exactly. Each pair splits into uppercase and lowercase.
  3. Separate touching letters. Thirteen pairs touch or overlap (C F K M N P R S T U V Y Z). No automatic rule separates R's leg from the r beside it, so those cuts were read off the source by eye and hard-coded.
  4. Rasterise per glyph. Each glyph is masked to its own strokes, so a touching neighbour cannot bleed into it.
  5. Trace. potrace converts each bitmap to Bézier outlines.
  6. Assemble. fontTools builds the CFF font: 1000 units/em, 700 cap height, 45-unit side bearings, plus a space glyph and cmap.
  7. Baselines. Measured bottoms scattered by up to a third of the cap height — noise, not handwriting. Every glyph is snapped to the baseline; g j p q y f keep the drop they were drawn with.
  8. Weight. The source pen is ~2px on capitals drawn 47px tall, which lands near 30 units/em and disappears at text sizes. Tall glyphs are thickened; the small lowercase are left alone, because the same step turns a 20px letter solid. Closing pinholes is size-aware for the same reason — a 3×3 close seals the counter of a e o at that size.
  9. Joins. Each glyph carries a short stroke on the baseline in its side bearings. Two adjacent letters put those end to end and the pen appears not to lift, which closes the gaps and reads as joined-up writing.
  10. Ship. Exported as .otf, .woff and .woff2.

Where it is used

Ask a question on the jam board and the answer is written out in Mynerve over the showreel, a character at a time. That surface is already live — when a real agent is connected, only answerFor() in script.js changes, and the replies arrive in Adi's handwriting automatically.

Known limits of the traced face

This joins printed handwriting; it does not restyle the letters into looped cursive, which would mean redrawing them. The source chart draws lowercase at roughly 15–20px, so e n o z carry little detail and read best above ~28px, and letters that touched in the source (c f p t especially) keep a trace of their neighbour's ink. There is no punctuation, no digits and no kerning — a comma or full stop falls back to the next font in the stack. A higher-resolution chart, and a second sheet with . , ? ! and 0–9, would fix most of this.
