01 — Personal finance · Product design

A budget you’ll actually keep.

Oykot Money turns a monthly budget into one clear daily answer: what is safe to spend today?

Role
Product design
Scope
Research, UX, UI system
Focus
Habit-building finance

Oykot Money’s product visual: a monthly dashboard that turns a plan into a clear view of income, expenses, and savings.

The brief

Make the monthly budget useful at the moment of a purchase.

How might we help people make better day-to-day spending decisions without making budgeting feel like work? Most budget tools are retrospective: they tell users what went wrong after the month has ended. Oykot Money was designed to make the next decision clearer, before the money leaves the account.

02 — The problem

Good intentions disappear when money management becomes another job.

01

Budgets are abandoned

Spreadsheets and complex dashboards demand too much setup and upkeep.

02

Balance is misleading

A bank balance does not account for rent, subscriptions, savings, or plans.

03

Overspending appears too late

End-of-month summaries explain the past, but cannot improve today’s choice.

04

Money is fragmented

Fixed bills, informal loans, assets, and accounts rarely live in one clear view.

03 — Who it is for

People building a more intentional relationship with money.

Early-career professionals planning their first salary.

Students and young adults building a spending habit.

People who use cash, UPI, cards, and multiple accounts.

Privacy-conscious users who prefer manual control to bank syncing.

04 — The insight

A budget fails when it only exists at the start and end of a month.

“I know I should save. I just don’t know what I can spend without messing up the rest of the month.”

Representative user tension
What we heard

People were not asking for another spreadsheet.

They wanted a quick sense check before ordering food, paying a bill, or saying yes to plans. Their financial information was already available—but it was not helping them choose in the moment.

The design opportunity

Turn the plan into a calm daily signal.

The product needed to make upcoming obligations visible, reduce the work of tracking, and translate a monthly budget into a number a user could confidently act on.

05 — Design process

Move from messy money behaviour to a repeatable, low-effort ritual.

  1. 01

    Understand the current habit

    Mapped how people track money today: mental maths, notes apps, spreadsheets, bank balances, and messages about borrowed money. The common pattern was reactive tracking after spending had already happened.

    Observe
  2. 02

    Define the decision to support

    Reframed the product around the question users return to most often: “What can I safely spend today?” This became the core product outcome, not simply a dashboard metric.

    Frame
  3. 03

    Design the money flow

    Created a simple sequence: set a monthly plan, account for recurring commitments, log what changes, then use a daily figure to guide the next decision.

    Simplify
  4. 04

    Build for return visits

    Used familiar language, light input moments, visible progress, and carry-forward planning so the budget feels like a small ongoing habit rather than a monthly admin task.

    Reinforce

06 — Product principle

From “what did I spend?” to “what can I spend?”

Every screen is designed to turn a broad financial plan into an understandable next action. Rather than show more numbers, the product creates a calm, dependable point of reference.

10 — The ongoing flow

A month-long experience, designed as seven small moments.

01

Create an account and begin the free trial.

02

Choose currency, date format, and monthly income.

03

Plan categories across Needs, Wants, and Investments.

04

Add recurring costs such as rent, subscriptions, SIPs, or EMIs.

05

Log spending and update money lent or borrowed.

06

Check the daily figure and pace before the next purchase.

07

Review the month, then carry the plan forward.

11 — What success looks like

First plan

More users complete their first monthly budget.

Early habit

Users continue logging after the first week.

Return value

Safe-to-spend becomes a frequently checked decision tool.

Long-term trust

Retention grows without relying on invasive data collection.

12 — Intended impact

Less financial anxiety. More everyday clarity.

Oykot Money does not promise investment advice or automated financial control. It offers a practical ritual: plan, notice, and adjust.
